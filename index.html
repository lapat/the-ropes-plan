The Space

Target: 3,000 sqft — meaningfully larger than 9Round's own footprint (their spec runs 1,500–2,500 sqft), so there's real room to add iPad infrastructure per station without cramping the circuit.

Criteria: New Jersey, near NYC, ideally near a train line, ideally near other gyms with an existing fight-sports following.

Recommendation: Central Avenue, Jersey City Heights

This corridor hits every criterion with real evidence behind it, not just proximity math.

  • Available now: 298 Central Ave (3,000 sqft, ~$6,000/mo) and 367 Central Ave (3,250–4,250 sqft) — both currently listed, both in range.
  • Existing fight-sports presence on the same street: Family Fitness Karate and Kickboxing runs a location at 419 Central Ave — direct evidence the corridor already supports a combat-sports business.
  • Broader Jersey City boxing/MMA scene nearby: World Boxing Gym, The Trinity Boxing Club, CULTR Fight Club, Heavy Hitters, CKO Kickboxing — a real, established market, not a cold start.
  • Transit: near the Journal Square PATH hub — not directly attached to a station, worth knowing honestly rather than oversold.

Alternative: 1 PATH Plaza has retail space literally inside a newly renovated PATH station, but the available units run larger (5,100+ sqft ground floor) — oversized for the target and further from the existing gym cluster.

Also checked: Hoboken and the waterfront. Real listings found there too (e.g. 74 Hudson St, 3,050 sqft at $78/sqft/yr), and Hoboken's transit is genuinely better — a true multimodal hub (PATH, NJ Transit, ferry, light rail all in one terminal, vs. Central Ave's HBLR-only access). But it runs roughly 2.7–3.3x the rent of Central Ave (likely $16,000–20,000/mo for a comparable space vs. $6,000/mo) and its combat-sports presence is thinner and more boutique-fitness (Rumble Boxing, CKO) rather than a real boxing/MMA cluster. Central Ave stays the pick — the rent gap is too large to justify for an unproven first location. Hoboken is worth revisiting later if this location succeeds and a second one makes sense.

Technology — Everything On the Table

Nothing here is decided. This is the real list of ideas being weighed, with an honest read on how proven or unproven each one is — not a single recommendation.

What's on the iPad at each station? Four real options

The open question that drives everything else. 9Round's own real model has no instructional screens at all — just a live trainer and a room-wide bell — which is the honest baseline to compare every idea below against. None of these are picked yet:

  1. Teddy shoots fresh content on a cadence. New videos weekly or monthly — real courses, Teddy just talking into a camera about what he's teaching that cycle. Teddy Jr then improvises off that material live on the floor, so it's always current, not one static library recorded once and never updated.
  2. Teddy's voice plus video, for technique specifically. An AI version of him — his real voice, possibly his face — walking someone through technique at a station. Unproven, may not work well. The voice (cloned from his own audio) is realistic with existing tools; the video/talking-face part is a harder, separate step that can look uncanny — worth prototyping and actually testing before betting on it.
  3. AI punch tracking. A live counter/gamified score from the camera. This part is solved and already working — see below.
  4. No app at all. Keep version one simple — pure 9Round model, screen adds nothing, all the teaching comes from Teddy Jr live on the floor.

Computer vision — what a camera can actually do

Researched in real depth, tested against real footage. Two very different pieces:

  • Punch counting — solved, achievable now. A working prototype already counts punches from real bag-work video using Apple's own on-device camera technology. No custom hardware needed.
  • Real technique coaching ("your chin's up," "rotate your hips") — a genuine, unsolved research problem. Even a $5.8M-funded company with a dedicated computer-vision team hasn't shipped this yet. Possible eventually, not a version-one bet.

Sensors and wearables

Custom bag or glove sensors (gamified punch data, à la FightCamp) are real hardware — something that has to be built, tested, and can break or feel janky, which is a genuine cost most software ideas don't carry. Off-the-shelf heart-rate wearables (the MYZONE approach 9Round and Orangetheory actually use) are safer — mature, proven, nothing to build.

Legal & Social Media Prep

Prep work that can start before the space is even secured, per the "sooner the better" priority.

Suggested order below — this is one reasonable sequence, not the only way to do it. Teddy and Teddy Jr (and Jay) should treat this as a starting checklist to adjust, not a script.

The actual steps, in order

  1. Jay decides: equity or gift/loan? Is the $250K a capital contribution (Jay/Louis get equity in a jointly-owned LLC) or a loan/gift to Teddy and Teddy Jr (no equity, they own it outright)? Nothing else below can really start until this is answered — it determines who the LLC's members are.
  2. Decide whose name the business runs under — the LLC, or Teddy/Teddy Jr personally — this follows directly from step 1.
  3. Form the NJ LLC. File a Certificate of Formation directly with NJ (~$125), not through an Illinois parent entity — simpler and cheaper. Jay's own company (Jennings Realty, Inc) is Illinois-based, in Evanston, so there's a real reason someone might default to routing through an Illinois structure — but since 100% of this gym's operations are in NJ, a direct NJ LLC still saves real money (~$150-450/yr) unless Jay specifically wants the gym folded into his existing Illinois corporate umbrella for his own tax/reporting reasons. Worth a direct question to him, not an assumption either way.
  4. Get an EIN and register with NJ-REG (tax/employer registration) — required before hiring anyone or collecting payment.
  5. Register with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs under the Health Club Services Act before opening — this is mandatory, not optional, for a business like this (see below). Decide the membership structure (month-to-month vs. long-term contracts) at this step, since it changes whether a surety bond is required.
  6. Get a real NJ attorney (not a template service) to draft: the operating/partnership agreement (Teddy Jr's role and pay, Jay/Louis's role), the name/likeness licensing agreement for using Teddy's name commercially, and the liability waiver — all three need to be specific to this business, not generic.
  7. Confirm "The Ropes" name directly with Teddy — not a trademark search anymore, a direct conversation, since he already owns it.
  8. Get general liability + contact-sport insurance quoted and bound before signing a lease — most landlords require proof of insurance before handing over keys.
  9. Tour and sign the lease (298/367 Central Ave or wherever's chosen), then get the Jersey City Commerce Division business license and Certificate of Occupancy for the fit-out.

NJ Health Club Services Act — a real requirement, not optional

New Jersey has a specific law (N.J.S.A. 56:8-39) covering any business devoting 40%+ of its space to fitness services — this gym qualifies. It requires registering with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs before opening, and if memberships run longer than 3 months, posting a surety bond (10% of prior-year gross income, $25K–$50K floor/ceiling). Members also get a mandatory 3-day cancellation right. A month-to-month membership model with no long-term contracts sidesteps the bonding requirement — worth deciding the membership structure with this in mind, not as an afterthought.

Jersey City also requires a separate local Commerce Division business license beyond state registration, plus a Certificate of Occupancy for the fit-out.

Legal documents — the actual links, and an honest answer on automation

Honest verdict first: there's no real way to auto-fill these. Every core NJ/Jersey City filing is a live government web form, not a downloadable fillable PDF, and none of them have a public API — checked GitHub directly, nothing exists for this. The AI-branded services (Rocket Lawyer, Firstbase, ZenBusiness, Clerky) are template generators with a human filing it for you, not automation — and for a plain single-state LLC like this, Reddit's small-business consensus is blunt: you don't need one, it's a 2-page form.

FilingWhere
NJ Certificate of Formationnjportal.com/businessformation/home — $125, same-day
EIN (IRS SS-4)irs.gov — free, instant, online interview tool. Skip any paid service offering to do this.
NJ-REG (tax/employer registration)njportal.com/DOR/BusinessRegistration — file after the Certificate of Formation is approved
NJ Health Club Services Act registrationnjconsumeraffairs.gov/healthclubs — also has a downloadable "Declaration of Exemption from Security Requirements" PDF, worth checking with a lawyer since month-to-month membership may qualify for exemption from the surety bond
Jersey City business license + Certificate of Occupancyjcnj.org/permitportal (GovPilot) — Commerce Division for the license, Construction Code for the CO, plus a required NJ Division of Fire Safety inspection

What's actually useful here: a pre-filled checklist of the exact values needed across every form (LLC name, registered agent name/address, NAICS code, business purpose language, principal address) so it's copy-paste into each portal instead of re-deriving it five times.
