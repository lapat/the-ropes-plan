The Space

Target: 3,000 sqft — meaningfully larger than 9Round's own footprint (their spec runs 1,500–2,500 sqft), so there's real room to add iPad infrastructure per station without cramping the circuit.

Criteria: New Jersey, near NYC, ideally near a train line, ideally near other gyms with an existing fight-sports following.

Recommendation: Central Avenue, Jersey City Heights

This corridor hits every criterion with real evidence behind it, not just proximity math.

  • Available now: 298 Central Ave (3,000 sqft, ~$6,000/mo) and 367 Central Ave (3,250–4,250 sqft) — both currently listed, both in range.
  • Existing fight-sports presence on the same street: Family Fitness Karate and Kickboxing runs a location at 419 Central Ave — direct evidence the corridor already supports a combat-sports business.
  • Broader Jersey City boxing/MMA scene nearby: World Boxing Gym, The Trinity Boxing Club, CULTR Fight Club, Heavy Hitters, CKO Kickboxing — a real, established market, not a cold start.
  • Transit: near the Journal Square PATH hub — not directly attached to a station, worth knowing honestly rather than oversold.

Alternative: 1 PATH Plaza has retail space literally inside a newly renovated PATH station, but the available units run larger (5,100+ sqft ground floor) — oversized for the target and further from the existing gym cluster.

Next step: this is as far as remote research can responsibly take it — touring 298/367 Central Ave with a broker is the actual next move.

Technology — Status By Piece

iPad-per-station app: the single most important piece of the tech plan, and still genuinely open. A first version (timer-driven station flow) was built and confirmed working, but paused after a real question surfaced: 9Round's actual model has no instructional screens at all — just a live trainer and a room-wide bell. What the screen is actually for is still being worked out before more app code gets written.

Computer-vision punch detection/coaching: researched in depth. Basic punch counting via Apple's own camera technology is real and achievable — a working prototype already counts punches from real bag-work footage. Real technique-quality coaching feedback ("your chin's up," "rotate your hips") is a genuine, unsolved research problem — even a $5.8M-funded specialist company with a dedicated computer-vision team hasn't shipped that yet. Call: don't build or promise AI coaching in version one. A lightweight, gamified punch-counter is a reasonable bonus feature; real coaching stays with Teddy Jr.

Teddy's digital twin: a voice clone only — no video avatar (ruled out as too strange). In progress, sourced from Teddy's YouTube and ESPN appearances.
